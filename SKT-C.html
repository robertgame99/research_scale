SKT-C

Chinese Version of the Stroke Knowledge Test (SKT-C)

Overview

The Chinese Version of the Stroke Knowledge Test (SKT-C) was translated, culturally adapted, and validated to assess stroke-related knowledge among patients with hypertension in Taiwan. The instrument evaluates patients' understanding of stroke, including risk factors, warning signs, prevention, treatment, and recovery. It provides healthcare professionals with a structured approach to identifying knowledge gaps and assessing educational needs related to stroke prevention.

Translation and Psychometric Validation

The SKT-C was developed through a systematic translation and cross-cultural adaptation process based on the original Stroke Knowledge Test (SKT), developed by Dr. Karen Sullivan. Written permission to translate the SKT was granted by Dr. Sullivan in June 2018.

The translation procedure followed Brislin's guidelines and included forward translation, back-translation, expert review, and assessment of face validity. Psychometric evaluation was conducted among 200 patients with hypertension recruited from an outpatient department of a regional hospital in Taiwan. An additional assessment of test-retest reliability was conducted among 30 participants after a two-week interval.

The final Chinese version retained all 20 items of the original instrument.

Instrument Structure and Scoring

The SKT-C consists of 20 multiple-choice questions assessing stroke-related knowledge. Each question provides five response options, including a "do not know" option. Correct responses are scored as 1 point, while incorrect and "do not know" responses are scored as 0 points. Total scores range from 0 to 20, with higher scores indicating greater stroke-related knowledge.

Reliability and Validity

The SKT-C demonstrated satisfactory content validity, with item-level content validity indices (I-CVI) ranging from 0.88 to 1.00 and a scale-level content validity index (S-CVI/Ave) of 0.97. The Kuder–Richardson Formula 20 (KR-20) coefficient was 0.62, indicating modest internal consistency, while the test-retest reliability coefficient was 0.77, suggesting acceptable temporal stability. Known-group validity was supported by significant differences in stroke knowledge according to educational attainment (F = 21.21, p < 0.001). Overall, these findings provide preliminary support for the reliability and validity of the SKT-C among patients with hypertension.

Research Applications

The SKT-C may be used to assess stroke-related knowledge among patients with hypertension, identify knowledge gaps regarding stroke risk factors and warning signs, and evaluate the effectiveness of stroke prevention education programs. It may also support patient education and health promotion interventions, as well as research examining factors associated with stroke knowledge in clinical and community settings. The instrument is particularly relevant to studies focusing on hypertension management, stroke prevention, and patient-centered health education.
