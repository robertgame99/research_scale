Nursing Cultural Competence Scale (NCCS)

Overview

The Nursing Cultural Competence Scale (NCCS) was developed to assess cultural competence among clinical nurses. The instrument addresses nurses' awareness of cultural differences, their ability to provide culturally appropriate nursing care, their use of cultural resources, and their capacity for self-directed cultural learning.

Development and Psychometric Validation

The NCCS was developed and validated in Taiwan using a two-phase research design. The initial item pool was informed by in-depth interviews with 30 nurses and evaluated by a panel of seven experts. Psychometric testing was subsequently conducted among 246 clinical nurses. The final 19-item scale comprises four dimensions: Cultural Awareness Ability, Cultural Action Ability, Cultural Resources Application Ability, and Self-Learning Cultural Ability.

Reliability and Validity

The four-factor structure explained 62.0% of the total variance. The NCCS demonstrated satisfactory internal consistency (Cronbach's α = 0.88) and test-retest reliability (r = 0.70), supporting its reliability for assessing cultural competence among clinical nurses.

Research Applications

The NCCS may be used to assess cultural competence among clinical nurses, identify professional development needs, and evaluate educational initiatives designed to strengthen culturally responsive nursing care. It may also support cross-cultural nursing research and the assessment of cultural competence in diverse healthcare settings.
