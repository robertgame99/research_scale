Nursing Shared Decision-Making Attitude Scale (NSDMA)

Overview

The Nursing Shared Decision-Making Attitude Scale (NSDMA) was developed to assess nurses' attitudes toward shared decision-making (SDM) in clinical practice. The instrument focuses on nurses' willingness to engage in patient-centered communication, respect patients' preferences and values, and develop the knowledge and skills needed to support collaborative healthcare decisions.

Development and Psychometric Validation

The NSDMA was developed through a two-phase instrument development study in Taiwan. The first phase involved qualitative interviews with 21 nurses and expert evaluation of item content. The second phase examined the instrument's psychometric properties among 430 clinical nurses, with an additional 100 nurses participating in the assessment used to establish an optimal cut-off score. The final 11-item instrument comprises two dimensions: Empathic Communication and Mastery Learning.

Reliability and Validity

The NSDMA demonstrated strong internal consistency, with an overall McDonald's omega coefficient of 0.92. Known-group validity was supported by significant differences associated with previous SDM training and experience. Receiver operating characteristic analysis identified an optimal cut-off score of 48.5.

Research Applications

The NSDMA may be used to assess nurses' attitudes toward SDM, identify educational needs, and evaluate the outcomes of SDM-related training programs. It may also support research on patient-centered care, interprofessional communication, and the implementation of shared decision-making in nursing practice.
